# Creek measurements v1 — prototype analytic contract

This reusable, offline analytic layer belongs to hill-country-hydro. It does not change the
schema-1 water bundle, source SQLite tables, raw records, rules or daily runner. The creek
website is one consumer. Python standard library only; no acquisition or metered calls.

## Reproduce

From the hydro repository, with its preserved local `data/raw/usgs/` and Hydromet current
captures, and committed/versioned daily and Hydromet history captures:

```sh
python3 tools/creek_normalize.py --storm 2026-09-30 --through 2026-10-03T12:15:00Z
python3 tools/creek_analytics.py
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests
```

`--root`, `--out`, `--input` and `--report` permit offline fixture/research copies. Missing
required captures or bad hashes fail loudly. USGS current captures are local archived inputs;
a fresh Git clone alone cannot recreate them. Restore the existing raw archive first. The
normalizer does not fetch replacements. Source paths/hashes and official request URLs are
retained in the catalog. The cutoff bounds an analysis snapshot, not a claim that each gauge
reported through that time. The recorded storm starts September 29 at 10:35 UTC despite its
September 30 filename; normalization includes one preceding hour for baseline selection.

## Outputs and reader interface

| Output | Contract |
|---|---|
| `data/normalized/creek-analytics/catalog.json` | `schema_version: creek-measurements/v1`; source catalog, series metadata, physical identities, display-series choices, retained conflicts, exact-time mirror checks and coverage. |
| `data/normalized/creek-analytics/observations.jsonl` | One original observation per line, including repeated captures, missing values and alternative sensors. Read directly or with `tools.creek_analytics.load(folder)`. The catalog records its SHA-256 and row count. |
| `data/normalized/creek-analytics/references.json` | Seasonal daily reference per selected USGS station, with contributing observation IDs and source IDs. Generated by `creek_analytics.py`. |
| `data/model/creek-normalization-audit.json` | Committed generated audit: every adapted series, observation counts by day/year, units, gaps, approval/qualifier counts, exclusions, Hydromet archive-window counts by year. |
| `docs/CREEK_COVERAGE.md` | Generated compact source/coverage table and comparability decisions. |

The large normalized store is ignored by the existing `data/normalized/` rule. The analytic
schema is versioned independently; breaking changes require a new version and changelog entry.
The website never needs to parse a provider-specific `value1` or choose a USGS sensor.

Each observation has:

- Stable `id` from the original feed-series, capture ID and record locator; original operator,
  distributing feed, site ID, series ID and parameter. `physical_gauge_id` keeps verified aliases
  together without merging series. Hydromet has no verified physical sensor IDs; its series
  identifies a feed/field/resolution, not a proven continuous instrument installation.
- Original and converted value/unit; separate quantity (`discharge`, `stage`, or unsupported
  `unknown`). Canonical discharge is ft³/s and stage is feet. Explicit conversion supports
  m³/s and metres. Stage remains distinct from elevation or depth; vertical datum is null.
- Original time string, normalized UTC instant **only when an offset is supplied**, or a
  date-only daily label. Daily `source_time` preserves the provider timestamp; no within-day
  measurement time or interval endpoints are invented from it. Unknown timezone and interval
  endpoints are null. `statistic` distinguishes instantaneous, daily_mean, reported_point,
  hourly_unspecified and unknown_daily. An hourly flag does not establish a mean.
- Original qualifiers and approval status (unknown when absent), `state`, explicit `issues`,
  retrieval time, source ID and exact `features/N/properties` or `records/N/field` locator.
  The source catalog resolves the raw/capture path, official URL, SHA-256 and retrieval time.
  Date labels are not point observations. Nonfinite values, negatives in this discharge scope,
  unknown units, rejected readings, ambiguous point times and provider-stale rows are unavailable.
  Missing values stay null; measured zero stays zero. Provisional readings remain marked.

## Identity and conflict policy

USGS agency plus the exact original USGS site number in Hydromet establishes a distribution
alias. Names and coordinates alone never establish that LCRA and USGS numbered sites share a
sensor. Both feeds remain intact; no average or stitched history is produced. Mirror diagnostics
compare exact instants and retain all matching native sensors, so disagreement with an alternate
sensor does not prove that the primary sensor disagrees.

A unique provider-designated primary USGS series wins the display selection. With only one
saved series, that series is selected with the missing designation disclosed. Multiple series
without a unique primary produce no selected series. The primary flags and gauge names/coordinates
come from each daily-history manifest and its original USGS time-series metadata capture.

Repeated captures of one series at one timestamp are revisions: the most recently retrieved
copy wins, even when missing/rejected. Every original and differing value remains available,
with a conflict flag on the resolved record. Retrieval-time ties break by capture ID and
observation ID for deterministic replay. No cross-series resolution occurs.

## Historical comparison and gaps

The reference is the median of Approved daily discharge from September 16–October 16 in
2006–2025, the twenty complete calendar years before the October 1 anchor. Each contributing
year needs at least 25 of 31 dates; the whole period needs at least 80% of 620 possible dates
and five qualifying years. Coverage counts, rejected partial years, zero share, qualifier-day
counts and all contributing IDs are published. Estimated but Approved values are retained and
counted. This is a historical reference, not a stationary-climate assumption or a flood threshold.

`seasonal_reference(rows)` never pools sensors. `contrast(reading, reference)` returns signed
instantaneous-minus-daily-median discharge, its explicitly named ratio if the median is positive,
and **no instantaneous historical percentile**. The daily median gives a usual day's flow;
it is not an instantaneous distribution. The contrast does not establish event rarity, return
period or a change in daily volume. Zero medians yield null ratios, never an artificial denominator.
Stage, unknown/hourly aggregation and insufficient history yield explicit unavailable results.

`at_or_before(rows, time)` returns the latest prior observation only within 30 minutes. It never
borrows a future value, interpolates across gaps, or skips a rejected latest observation to
resurrect an older one. Event peaks must be called **largest saved readings**, since gaps can
hide a larger peak. A multi-gauge peak view is not a simultaneous snapshot.

## Bounded scope

Six USGS gauges: Bull at Loop 360, Barton at Loop 360, Shoal at W 12th, Walnut at Webberville,
Williamson at Jimmy Clay, Onion at US 183. Seven comparison feeds: LCRA 3992/4520/4561/4598 and
City 122/2400/950. Their event flow/stage and USGS daily flow are normalized. Hydromet long-history
coverage comes from captured-window manifests, not a claim of complete numeric records.
Blunn has City flow but no chosen daily reference. City Shoal and Williamson have no numeric
event-history flow. Stage datum continuity, historical sensor continuity, rainfall, lakes,
springs and wells are outside this adapter. Saved geometry is a separate product input;
normalization itself never asserts a gauge-to-reach relationship.

Hydromet field labels are evidenced by the preserved official report under
`data/captures/documentary/hydromet-normalization/`, plus each capture's Stage/Flow headers.
The report labels stage in feet and flow in cfs. No external metadata is needed on replay.
